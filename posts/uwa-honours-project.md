## 117. Smart camera-based monitoring of laboratory animal activity
- **Supervisor**: [Prof Girish Dwivedi](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/persons/girish-dwivedi/)
- **Contact**: [girish.dwivedi@perkins.uwa.edu.au](mailto:girish.dwivedi@perkins.uwa.edu.au)
- **Majors**: [A&RE, BiomedE, EEE]
- **Max Students**: 1

### Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills
This project suits mechatronics, electrical/electronic or biomedical engineering students who enjoy hands-on work, have basic programming skills and are willing to learn new techniques: Basic Python programming and an interest in camera-based tracking; Familiarity with electronics or platforms such as Arduino or ESP32; Willingness to assemble, test and troubleshoot a prototype; Experience with CAD, 3D printing or AI is helpful but not essential.

### Project Description
Animal studies are an important step in testing new drugs, materials and medical devices before human studies. Monitoring animals in their cages helps researchers understand their responses to treatments and identify changes in health or behaviour. This project aims to make monitoring easier and more consistent while reducing handling and disturbance. The student will help build and test a camera-based tracking system, assemble cameras and electronics, develop software to track movement, and evaluate performance under different conditions.

## 17. Soil sensing over long distances using time domain reflectometry
- **Supervisor**: [Dr Adrian Keating](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/persons/adrian-keating/)
- **Contact**: [adrian.keating@uwa.edu.au](mailto:adrian.keating@uwa.edu.au)
- **Majors**: [A&RE, EEE, MechE]
- **Max Students**: 1

### Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills
Python, understanding of time and frequency domain signals.

### Project Description
The goal of this project is to evaluate soil sensors and calibrate them using a newly developed time domain reflectometry (TDR) system. The system uses a vector network analyser which is programmed via Python. The goal is to  extract data over long distances (potentially 500m with many sensors) and over time (many weeks) to determine if this approach can yield reliable data. After initial calibration and testing, opportunities to work on field testing is available.

## 121. Live health tracking of power transformers for maximization of grid performance and failure prevention
- **Supervisor**: [Prof Victor Sreeram](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/persons/victor-sreeram/)
- **2nd Supervisor**: [Hesam Mohammadi](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hesam-mohammadi-29314a213/)
- **Contact 1**: [victor.sreeram@uwa.edu.au](mailto:victor.sreeram@uwa.edu.au)
- **Contact 2**: [hesam.mohammadi@uwa.edu.au](mailto:hesam.mohammadi@uwa.edu.au)
- **Majors**: [EEE]
- **Max Students**: 1

### Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills
Completion of ELEC3016 Power and Machines

### Project Description
This project develops an intelligent virtual replica of a power transformer that acts like a real-time health tracker, continuously monitoring its condition much like a smartwatch tracks a person's vital signs. By evaluating live health data and surrounding temperatures, the digital twin calculates a real-time health score, allowing an automated computer program to safely unlock extra power delivery during high-demand surges without overworking the machine. This replaces outdated, rigid manufacturer guidelines with custom, health-driven limits, enabling utility companies to maximize their existing equipment, precisely predict when maintenance is needed, and prevent power interruptions.

## 43. Compact low-cost laser hair removal system
- **Supervisor**: [A/Prof Barry Cense](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/persons/barry-cense/)
- **Contact**: [barry.cense@uwa.edu.au](mailto:barry.cense@uwa.edu.au)
- **Majors**: [A&RE, BiomedE, EEE, MechE, SoftE]
- **Max Students**: 4

### Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills
N/A

### Project Description
This project aims to develop a low-cost, handheld laser hair removal system capable of automatically identifying and targeting individual hairs on human legs (based on expired US Patent 7,108,690 B1). The system will combine optical imaging, image processing, and laser beam steering to selectively target hair follicles while minimising exposure of the surrounding skin. A camera captures images of the skin. Image-processing algorithms will identify individual hairs and estimate their follicle locations. A repurposed barcode scanner will then direct a focused near infra red laser diode beam towards the selected targets. Wheels integrated into the handheld device will measure its movement across the skin, enabling continuous tracking and compensation for changes in position. The student team will design, build, and evaluate a functional prototype. A custom PCB will incorporate the laser driver, galvanometer driver, and a microcontroller such as the ESP32, coordinating image acquisition, target recognition, beam positioning, and motion tracking. The project offers opportunities to collaborate on an interdisciplinary engineering challenge, with the ultimate goal of demonstrating the feasibility of an affordable, intelligent, and compact hair removal device.
